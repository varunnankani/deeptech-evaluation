The underwriting mechanism

General-purpose SAR already exists. PierSight must win on maritime economics, persistent observation and the value created above raw imagery.

01 / WHITE SPACE

Persistent maritime monitoring

Large ocean areas still make continuous surveillance a difficult systems problem.

02 / WHY NOW

2023-25 policy inflection

Private participation, FDI reform, dedicated venture capital and sovereign EO procurement moved from policy intent into investable infrastructure.

03 / WHAT MUST BE TRUE

Architecture has to matter

The maritime-specific design must create measurable cost, persistence or workflow advantages.

04 / INTELLIGENCE LAYER

MATSYA must exceed imagery

Customers need to act on fused intelligence and pay recurring subscriptions or API fees.

05 / KILL CRITERION

Do not remain capacity-bound

If incremental revenue consistently requires similar satellite capex, the economics stay linear.

If intelligence revenue grows faster than satellite count, the observation layer is being monetised repeatedly. If every incremental dollar needs similar new satellite capex, the model remains capacity-bound.

These questions are designed to test the economics behind the constellation and the eventual value of MATSYA.

01

At what constellation scale does recurring intelligence revenue meaningfully switch on?

02

What measurable advantage does the maritime-specific architecture create versus ICEYE or Capella?

03

How many paid workflows can reuse the same underlying observation?

04

What does MATSYA gain from PierSight's own data that it cannot get from third-party feeds?

05

How much data can PierSight retain and reuse to improve models under sovereign and commercial contracts?

06

What would have to be true for MATSYA to become the larger source of enterprise value than the satellite network?
